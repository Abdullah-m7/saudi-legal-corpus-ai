## Option B — the question first

Leads with the problem rather than the dataset. Strongest if you want the
biography to argue for the work.

> Abdullah Almohammedi studies a question most legal research takes for
> granted: whether a state's own published law can be relied upon. To answer
> it he built the Saudi Legal Corpus, an open article-level record of Saudi
> Arabian legislation in which every verified article carries the sources it
> was reconciled from, and uses it to measure availability, consistency and
> amendment in the official record. He is an independent researcher based in
> Rabigh, Saudi Arabia, trained in law, and publishes the data and code
> behind every figure he reports.

## Option C — short and plain

Leaves the most room if you want to add credentials, and reads as confident
rather than sparse.

> Abdullah Almohammedi is an independent researcher in legal informatics,
> based in ⟨city⟩, Saudi Arabia. He built the Saudi Legal Corpus, an open
> article-level record of Saudi Arabian legislation, and uses it to measure
> how reliably states publish and maintain their own law. He publishes his

## What stays out of all three, and why

**Where the papers are under review.** Six manuscripts, five with journals,
none accepted. A biography that lists submissions reads as padding, and a
reviewer who checks finds nothing published.

**"AI-assisted" or any framing of method.** The generative-AI declaration
covers it in full, in its own section, where a reader expects to find it.
Repeating it here invites it to be read as identity rather than disclosure.

**Any claim about being first, or about a gap in the literature.** Neither is
verifiable from this repository, and both are the kind of claim a reviewer
tests first.
