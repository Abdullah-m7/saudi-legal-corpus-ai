# Claim: the statute book as an empirical unit of legal reasoning

A claim file, not a manuscript. It states what would be argued, what carries
it, what would defeat it, and what is not yet good enough — so that the
decision to write can be taken later against a record rather than a memory.

## The claim

Within one court system, one body of judges and one procedural law, different
statute books stand in **systematically different relationships to
non-statutory legal authority**, and the difference is large, stable under the
obvious controls, and not reducible to any measurable property of the codes
themselves.

The contribution is not that Saudi codes differ. It is that **the statute book
is an empirical unit of legal reasoning** — that "how much law from outside
the text does this body of enacted rules attract, and of what kind" is a
measurable property with an order-of-magnitude range inside a single
jurisdiction. Saudi Arabia is the setting because it has nine recent codes
applied by the same commercial chambers within a five-year window, which makes
the comparison possible; the object is general.

## What carries it

1. **Range.** Court supplementation runs from 4.1 per cent (Arbitration Law)
   to 59.4 (Law of Practice) across 30,000 judgments of 1444–1446.
2. **Not exposure.** Standardising every code to the corpus distribution of
   citation counts moves the extremes by under two points.
3. **Not crude case mix.** Within judgments that all cite the Commercial
   Courts Law, citing the Arbitration Law still goes with a 36-point fall;
   every marginal effect keeps its sign, and the largest grows.
4. **Not the text.** Leave-one-instrument-out prediction from nine mechanical
   textual features gives MAE 15.3 against a null of 17.2. Explicit Shariah
   reference in a code's own articles correlates with fiqh citation beside it
   at r = +0.01.
5. **Composition, not just level.** The profiles differ in kind: the Evidence
   Law draws named jurists at ten times its maxim rate; the Civil Transactions
   Law is the only major code whose maxim and custom rates approach its
   named-fiqh rate; the Law of Practice draws settled judicial practice at
   22.9 per cent.
6. **Voice.** Procedural codes are supplemented by the bench, substantive
   codes by the bar — the Civil Transactions Law is 24.9 per cent supplemented
   in the court's voice and 50.9 in the litigant's.
7. **A downstream consequence that is not rhetorical.** Statute-only retrieval
   risk is code-specific across an order of magnitude, and the traceability of
   what is added ranges from 69.9 per cent (Sharia Procedure implementing
   regulation) to 11.1 (Arbitration).

## What would defeat it

- ~~**Within-code variance.**~~ **Answered.** Three quarters of the variance
  in article-level supplementation is inside codes, which looked like the
  strongest objection: the ecology might be nothing but the mix of article
  types each code contains. Tested and rejected. Chance-corrected, instrument
  identity carries as much as a 34-cell article scheme (+18.9 against +18.1)
  with a quarter of the cells; it adds 14.9 per cent of the residual after
  article properties are fitted first; and two articles doing the same
  adjudicative work in the same citation band differ by a median 20.4 points
  across codes against 11.0 within one — 19.8 against 7.4 among
  dispute-deciding articles. The code is a defensible unit. What remains true
  is that it is not the *only* unit: article properties explain as much again,
  and the two are largely orthogonal.
- **One extreme is one article.** 256 of the Law of Practice's 345 judgments
  are its fee provision. Any claim that rests on the top of the range rests on
  a single open-textured standard.
- **Instability.** The Evidence Law falls from 46.0 to 30.9 per cent across
  four years and the Companies Law from 49.3 to 9.8. A property that moves
  that fast in three years is not a settled characteristic of a code.
- **Thin cells.** Arbitration 363 judgments, Civil Transactions 357, Companies
  634, Bankruptcy 86. The negative extreme that does most of the theoretical
  work is one of the thinner ones.
- **Publication.** Everything is published commercial judgments. If the
  publisher's selection differs by subject matter, part of the between-code
  difference is editorial, and this corpus cannot separate that.

## What is not claimed

That codes *cause* their ecologies. That an unsupplemented code is better
drafted. That non-citation is non-application. That fiqh is receding — the
five-denominator view says it is diluted relative to statute and flat per
reasoned judgment.

## Decision

**WRITE, and not yet — but the case is materially stronger than when this
file was opened.** The objection that would have sunk it, that the ecology is
just the article mix, has been tested and rejected. The four conditions set
for a second paper are met:
the effect is strong, it is not trivial composition, the mixes genuinely
differ, and the novelty survives a literature check that found the object
(statute books compared against each other) absent from a literature that
compares systems, courts and cases. What is missing before drafting is not
more analysis of this corpus but a decision about the frame — whether the
paper is about Saudi codification or about the statute book as a unit, since
the second is the contribution and the first is the evidence.

Working title, not fixed: *One Legal System, Several Authority Ecologies*.
