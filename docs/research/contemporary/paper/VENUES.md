# Venue shortlist

Assessed after the manuscript, on fit rather than impact factor. **Nothing
submitted, nobody contacted.** Details below need confirming on each journal's
current site before any submission — fees and word limits change.

## PRIMARY

**1 · Journal of Empirical Legal Studies (JELS)** — Wiley, for Cornell/SELS.
The natural home: the paper is a measurement-validity argument with a large-n
empirical demonstration, which is JELS's core. Peer-reviewed, takes
non-US settings, no submission fee typical of law reviews. *Caveat: this
project already has a manuscript in the JELS pipeline (MS 4582420); a second
concurrent submission needs the editor's tolerance and should be disclosed.*

**2 · Journal of Law and Courts** — Cambridge, for the Law and Courts section
of APSA. Publishes judicial-behaviour and citation work, and has published on
how briefs enter opinions, which is the nearest neighbour to this paper.
Double-blind. Good fit for the negative-controls framing.

**3 · Artificial Intelligence and Law** — Springer. The implication for legal
NLP and retrieval is a first-class contribution there, and the venue
understands rhetorical-role segmentation without needing it explained. Would
want the benchmark pilot appended, which is a reason to keep `BENCHMARK.md`
warm.

## SECONDARY

**4 · International Journal for Court Administration** — open access, no fees,
explicitly comparative and receptive to non-Western judiciaries. Lower
visibility in the empirical-legal community; strong fit for the Saudi setting
and for an independent researcher.

**5 · Journal of Legal Analysis** — Harvard, open access, no fee, methodological
work welcome. High bar; the paper would need the replication in question 1 of
the backlog to compete.

**6 · Law, Technology and Humans** — open access, no fees, publishes
computational-legal work with methodological framing.

## FALLBACK

**7 · JURIX / ICAIL proceedings** — peer-reviewed venues for AI-and-law. Would
reach the audience that most needs implication VIII.B and VIII.C. Shorter
format forces the paper to drop the Saudi context, which is a real loss.

**8 · Preprint (SSRN + arXiv cs.CL)** with a stated intention to submit. This
project already uses preprints; the paper is complete enough to post and the
speaker-attribution point is one others could use immediately.

## Selection criteria applied

- **empirical-legal fit** — 1, 2, 5 strongest
- **computational-law fit** — 3, 6, 7 strongest
- **comparative / non-Western acceptance** — 2, 4, 6 explicit; 1 and 5 in
  practice
- **independent-researcher eligibility** — all; 4, 5, 6 charge nothing
- **blinding** — 2 double-blind, which suits a paper whose author has no
  institution
- **word limit** — the draft is ~3,500 words and fits everywhere; JELS and JLA
  would expect it longer, with the robustness tables in the body

## Recommendation

**JELS primary, Journal of Law and Courts secondary, AI & Law third**, with a
preprint posted on completion regardless. The concurrent-submission question
at JELS is the user's call, not a scheduling detail.
