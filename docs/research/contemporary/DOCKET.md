# Is it the books, or is it the disputes?

Everything the instrument effect had survived was a property of the text or of
the citation. The last large competitor was the case itself: the codes may
simply be invoked in different kinds of dispute. This is that test.

**Frame decision: C — CODE_EFFECT_SURVIVES_DOCKET.** Dispute composition
explains a real and in one case large part of the spread, and the differences
between codes survive standardising to a common docket, matching judgments
that look alike, and holding reasons length fixed. The stronger claim (D)
holds for the Evidence Law — and the construct check on the measurement unit
shows that contrast is a *lower* bound — but it cannot be established
generally: the negative case is untestable inside a judgment and the positive
extreme reverses. And the honest counterweight is that in a prediction test
the docket carries 87.2 per cent of the improvement over a grand mean and the
codes 12.8. Those answer different questions and both are reported.

## 1 · What the corpus can actually see about a dispute

Thirteen flags read from the **recital only** — the part that states the
parties' requests and what happened procedurally, before the court reasons.
That ordering is the whole design: a feature read out of the reasons would be
a symptom of supplementation, not a description of the case.

Each was validated against a target it was never built from — whether the
judgment cites the article that matches it — reported as the gap between
flagged and unflagged judgments:

|---|---:|---:|---|

| expert | 2,771 | +15.1 (cites an expert-appointment article) | VALID |

The composition is real and it is concentrated exactly where the extremes
are. The Law of Practice sits in fee claims 61.8 per cent of the time against
a corpus base of 25.1; the Companies Law in corporate claims 79.5 against
23.0; the Bankruptcy Law is further from the corpus claim mix than any other
code, 0.831 on a scale where 1 is disjoint. The Arbitration Law's judgments
are the **shortest** in the corpus, median 1,237 characters of reasons against
1,540, which matters because short reasons carry less of everything.

The two largest codes are almost indistinguishable from the corpus — the
Commercial Courts Law at 0.021, the Sharia Procedure Law at 0.051. **So the
competitor is not imaginary. It is strongest precisely at the ends of the
range the claim rests on.**

## 3 · Standardising every code to a common docket

Direct standardisation over 72 strata — claim family × contestedness ×
reasons-length band — to the corpus-wide mix:

| instrument | n | raw | standardised | delta | strata coverage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| law practice law | 346 | 59.5 % | 48.0 % | −11.5 | 45.8 % |
| commercial implementing regulation | 3,911 | 48.6 % | **32.8 %** | **-15.8** | 95.9 % |
| evidence law | 7,702 | 39.5 % | 34.9 % | −4.6 | 98.5 % |
| commercial courts law | 13,410 | 37.2 % | 34.6 % | −2.6 | 99.9 % |
| sharia procedure law | 3,897 | 28.1 % | 28.2 % | +0.1 | 99.5 % |
| civil transactions law | 360 | 24.7 % | 26.3 % | +1.6 | 51.4 % |
| companies law | 638 | 15.8 % | 20.5 % | +4.7 | 40.6 % |
| arbitration law | 369 | 4.3 % | 5.2 % | +0.9 | 43.1 % |
| bankruptcy law | 88 | 3.4 % | 3.6 % | +0.2 | **4.4 %** |

Two things happen at once, and both matter.

**The four codes with near-complete strata coverage converge.** Raw they run
28.1 to 48.6; standardised, 28.2 to 34.9. The implementing regulation loses
almost sixteen points and lands beside the Commercial Courts Law. That gap was
docket composition, and the earlier reading of it as a code property was
wrong.

**The extremes do not move.** Arbitration 4.3 → 5.2, bankruptcy 3.4 → 3.6, and
the Law of Practice stays near fifty. The seventeen-fold raw spread becomes a
thirteen-fold standardised one. But the coverage column is the honest caveat:
the small codes sit in strata the corpus barely contains, so under half of
their judgments can be standardised at all, and bankruptcy at 4.4 per cent
coverage is not standardised in any meaningful sense.

## 6 · The strongest test: two codes inside one judgment

Where a single judgment cites two codes, the dispute, the chamber, the year,
the parties, the facts and the document are held fixed by construction. The
whole-judgment flag is useless there — it marks every code the moment one
jurist is quoted anywhere — so what is measured is whether a non-statutory
mention falls within a window of *that code's own citations*. It is called
**local non-statutory co-authority**: co-occurrence at a distance, not
supplementation. Three windows, so the reader can see what depends on the
choice. 3,735 multi-code judgments that carry non-statutory authority
somewhere.

| instrument | mentions | ±500 chars | ±1000 | same block |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| evidence law | 3,164 | **55.4 %** | **85.1 %** | **36.0 %** |
| civil transactions law | 111 | 50.5 % | 78.4 % | 35.1 % |
| commercial implementing reg | 1,913 | 53.4 % | 77.7 % | 24.1 % |
| sharia procedure law | 836 | 35.2 % | 74.6 % | 33.7 % |
| commercial courts law | 5,159 | 33.8 % | 65.8 % | 27.8 % |
| companies law | 120 | 43.3 % | 68.3 % | 25.8 % |
| law practice law | 186 | **23.7 %** | 46.2 % | 23.1 % |
| arbitration law | **6** | — | — | — |

Pairwise inside the same judgment, the share of judgments in which the first
code's citations sit nearer non-statutory authority than the second's:

| contrast | judgments | ±500 | same block |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| evidence law over commercial courts law | 2,137 | **74.9 %** | **60.8 %** |
| evidence law over sharia procedure law | 189 | 74.8 % | 65.9 % |
| evidence law over law practice law | 60 | 83.7 % | 84.2 % |
| implementing regulation over commercial courts law | 1,354 | 74.0 % | 50.4 % |
| implementing regulation over sharia procedure law | 294 | 84.5 % | 29.5 % |
| civil transactions over commercial courts law | 67 | 65.3 % | 66.7 % |

**The Evidence Law result is the strongest single piece of evidence in the
programme.** In 2,137 judgments that cite both, the Evidence Law's own
citations sit nearer the fiqh than the Commercial Courts Law's in three
quarters of them at ±500 characters and three fifths at the sentence-block
level. Same dispute, same chamber, same paragraph budget — and the two codes
sit at different distances from the non-statutory authority in the room.

## 6b · Does "near" mean "about the same thing"?

Section 6 rests on a measurement decision — that a fiqh mention within N
characters of a statutory citation belongs to the same piece of reasoning —
and that decision was made by sensitivity analysis alone. Agreement between
windows is not the same as the windows measuring what they claim to. So 63
citation neighbourhoods were drawn from 20 judgments citing both codes, and
the passages were read: **is the nearest non-statutory authority part of the
same reasoning proposition as this statutory citation?**

Of the 28 that fall inside ±500 characters:

| | n | related | ambiguous | unrelated |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all | 28 | **42.9 %** | 17.9 % | 39.3 % |
| beside an Evidence Law citation | 15 | **60.0 %** | 26.7 % | 13.3 % |
| beside a Commercial Courts Law citation | 13 | **23.1 %** | 7.7 % | 69.2 % |

**As an absolute measure the window is poor** — fewer than half the pairs
inside it are clearly one proposition — and the local rates in section 6 must
not be read as rates of connected reasoning.

**But the error is not symmetric, and it runs against the finding.** Beside a
procedural article the nearby fiqh is usually about something else: art. 16
confers jurisdiction and the nearest authority, three hundred characters
later, is كشاف القناع on مماطلة for the fee claim at the end of the judgment.
Beside an Evidence Law article it is usually the same point: «استقر العرف
التجاري على اعتبار أن التوقيع على الفواتير… دلالة على استلام ما جاء في
مضمونها» sits against art. 29 on the weight of a signed document, and the
hadith «لو يعطى الناس بدعواهم» restates art. 3 in the same sentence.

Correcting for relatedness would therefore **widen** the Evidence-over-
Commercial-Courts gap, not close it. The reported contrast is a lower bound.

## 6c · How much does the code add once the case is known?

Standardisation and matching compare codes to each other. The prediction
question is different: given a judgment, how much better can its
supplementation be anticipated from the codes it cites, over what the kind of
case already says? Two models fitted as group means on half the judgments and
scored on the other half, with a cluster bootstrap over judgments because one
judgment supplies the outcome for every code it cites.

| model | Brier | improvement |
|---|---:|---:|
| grand mean only | 0.20576 | — |
| **MODEL 0** — docket stratum alone | 0.18093 | +0.02483 |
| **MODEL 1** — docket plus the codes cited | 0.17728 | **+0.00364** (95 % CI +0.00236 to +0.00499) |

The code adjustments are real, ordered as the rest of the programme would
predict, and span twenty-five points: law practice +0.095, implementing
regulation +0.083, evidence +0.047, commercial courts +0.046, sharia procedure
−0.000, companies −0.1037, bankruptcy −0.1518, arbitration -0.1547, civil
transactions −0.1602.

**And they are the smaller part of the story: the codes carry 12.8 per cent
of the total improvement over the grand mean, the docket the other 87.2.**
This is the strongest single limitation on the claim, and it is not in tension
with sections 3 to 6. Those ask how two codes differ; this asks how well a
judgment can be anticipated. The kind of case is the better predictor of a
judgment. The statute book is still the thing that differs between two codes
in the same case.

## 7 · What kills parts of the claim

The adversarial pass, and it lands.

**The Law of Practice reverses.** Raw it is the most supplemented code at 59.5
per cent; locally its citations are the **farthest** from non-statutory
authority of any code measured, 23.7 per cent against the Commercial Courts
Law's 33.8, and the Evidence Law outranks it inside shared judgments 84 per
cent of the time. The reconciliation is visible in the fee stress test: a fee
claim is hybrid 35.5 per cent of the time without the Law of Practice and 66.4
with it, but the fiqh in those judgments is the مماطلة and costs material that
sits beside art. 164 of the implementing regulation, not beside the fee
article. **"The Law of Practice ecology" is a property of fee judgments, not
of the code's own text.** The phrase "nine separate settlements with fiqh",
used in an earlier report, over-claims and is withdrawn.

**The implementing regulation is window-dependent.** It leads the Commercial
Courts Law 74.0 per cent of the time at ±500 characters and 50.4 — exactly
chance — at the block level, and its standardised rate falls by sixteen
points. Its apparent ecology is substantially docket composition plus
proximity at a coarse window.

**The negative case cannot be tested where it matters.** The Arbitration Law
appears in multi-code judgments with six citations in total. Everything strong
about arbitration — 4.3 per cent raw, 5.2 standardised, 9.1 even in the
longest reasons, gaps of 22 to 44 points that survive matching — is
between-judgment evidence. It is never observed beside another code in the
same paragraph, so the cleanest test is silent on it.

**Bankruptcy is not standardisable.** 4.4 per cent strata coverage. Its 3.4
per cent is reported and nothing is built on it.

## 8 · The Evidence Law: the code, not the proof dispute

If the Evidence Law's ecology were really a property of disputes about proof,
proof disputes without the code should look like proof disputes with it:

| | n | hybrid |
|---|---:|---:|
| cites the Evidence Law, proof dispute in the recital | 3,089 | 40.4 % |
| cites the Evidence Law, no proof marker | 4,613 | 39.0 % |
| proof dispute, does **not** cite the Evidence Law | 5,358 | 26.4 % |
| neither | 10,566 | 21.9 % |

Citing the code adds about fourteen points whether or not the recital shows a
proof dispute; having a proof dispute without the code adds four and a half.
**The ecology travels with the statute book, not with the subject matter.**

## 9 · Where the variation lives

Judgment-level, chance-corrected, because a grouping with more cells explains
more by arithmetic:

| grouping | groups | share | chance | excess |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| the set of codes cited | 289 | 16.05 % | 1.22 | **+14.83** |
| docket stratum (all three) | 72 | 12.50 % | 0.30 | **+12.20** |
| reasons-length band | 4 | 8.84 % | 0.01 | +8.83 |
| claim family | 6 | 2.46 % | 0.02 | +2.44 |
| city | 22 | 1.03 % | 0.09 | +0.94 |
| contestedness | 3 | 0.71 % | 0.01 | +0.70 |
| year | 3 | 0.16 % | 0.01 | +0.15 |

Which codes a judgment cites and what kind of dispute it is are close, +14.83
against +12.20, and both dwarf year, city and contestedness. This is the
honest summary of the session: **the dispute matters about as much as the
statute book, and neither substitutes for the other.**

|---|---:|---:|

Even at a common docket mix the range is 5.2 to 48.0 per cent. **Retrieval
policy can be code-aware and not merely dispute-aware**, though the honest
version is weaker than the raw table suggested: for the four large procedural
and evidentiary codes the standardised rates sit within seven points of each
other, and the wide range is carried by arbitration at one end and the fee
code at the other.
