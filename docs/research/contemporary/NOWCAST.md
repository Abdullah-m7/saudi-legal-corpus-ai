# What is happening in Saudi law now

The repository's mission changes here. Reconstructing the past stops being the
object and becomes baseline, calibration and negative control. Every frozen
era, forecast, signature and registry is preserved untouched; none of it is
the programme any more.

**One thing has to be said before any number.** "Now" means the latest MATURE
quarter of the published record — **1446Q1**, roughly **2024-07** — while the
session clock reads 2026. So everything below is *current-as-published*, not
*current-as-decided*, and the gap between the two is not measurable because no
publication date exists per judgment. That is now a **standing limitation**,
recorded and moved past, not a research programme.

## 3. What is pressing on the system

21 pressure signals, all **OBSERVED** — 18 at the NOW horizon, 3 at NEAR. The
three classes are never mixed: OBSERVED is measured here or quoted from a held
enacted text; EXPECTED follows from an observed fact plus a stated mechanism;
SPECULATIVE has neither. **A horizon is not a forecast**; it says when a
consequence could plausibly emerge, with no probability attached.

Seven come from a bounded current official pass — 2026 designated the Year of
Artificial Intelligence; SDAIA's draft Responsible AI policy under public
consultation; PDPL licensing and accreditation rules; PDPL penalty committees
operating; non-Saudi real-estate ownership in force from 22 January 2026;
Labour Law amendments (38 articles revised, 7 omitted, 2 added); the new
Enforcement Law. The rest are measured inside the corpus.

**And the honest summary of the frontier:** the two most active current
frontiers — AI governance and data protection — are the two **least observable
here**, because their forum is regulatory, not commercial adjudication.

## 4. AI already has explicit anchors in this forum's own law

This is the strongest finding of the session, and it comes from enacted texts
the repository already held.

> **اللائحة التنفيذية لنظام المحاكم التجارية، المادة الرابعة والعشرون**
> «يجوز الاستفادة من تقنيات الذكاء الاصطناعي في الإجراءات الإلكترونية،
> ويستغنى عن أي إجراء تحققت غايته باستخدام تلك التقنية.»

> **الأدلة الإجرائية لنظام الإثبات، المادة الثالثة والعشرون**
> «يجوز الاستعانة بالتقنيات الحديثة في إجراءات الإثبات، بما في ذلك الذكاء
> الاصطناعي، ويُستغنى عن أي إجراء تحققت غايته باستخدام هذه التقنيات.»

Two more permit AI in notarisation (اللائحة التنفيذية لنظام التوثيق، م.20) and
in enforcement services (لائحة مقدمي خدمات التنفيذ، م.16), and SDAIA's
organisational arrangements make it the national authority for AI.

Seven anchors in all, **five EXPLICIT_RULE**, every one quoted verbatim with
its instrument and article. And the distinction that matters:

**Saudi law already permits AI in the procedures of the very forum this corpus
observes, and the corpus contains zero disputes in which AI is material.
Permission is present; consequence is untested.**

Nothing in the map asserts that an instrument applies. It records where the
system would have to absorb a problem, not how it would resolve one.

## 5. How much could existing law already absorb?

17 AI issue families, each with its current status, its anchors, its likely
entry point, its first observable signal and its watch condition.

| gap class | families |
|---|--:|
| UNCLEAR | 5 |
| GENERAL_RULE | 4 |
| PROCEDURAL_ONLY_ENTRY | 3 |
| EXPLICIT_RULE | 2 |
| NO_OBVIOUS_ANCHOR | 2 |
| ANALOGICAL_ENTRY_POINT | 1 |

**A missing AI statute is not a gap.** The question was never whether Saudi
Arabia needs an AI law; it was how much of the emerging problem the existing
system can already take. The answer on these texts: evidence and procedure are
covered explicitly, contract and liability arrive through general rules, and
**liability allocation for autonomous agents is the family the held texts do
not address in these words** — a statement about the texts held, not a finding
that Saudi law is silent.

Two of the sharpest families are not observable here at all. Automated public
decisions and data liability belong to the administrative and regulatory
forums; this corpus is Ministry of Justice commercial adjudication.

## 6. First-AI-case readiness

The capture schema is defined **now**, against a base rate of zero, so the
first validated AI-material dispute is recorded against a fixed template
rather than described after the fact: date, quarter, domain, issue family,
party and court legal anchors, Evidence Law and CTL involvement, non-statutory
authority, new doctrinal source, new statutory interpretation, court/bar
divergence, traceability, formula novelty, and whether the existing anchor was
enough.

Forbidden in the same breath: inferring AI from writing style, treating a
mention as a material dispute, predicting an outcome, naming anyone.

## 7. What was issued, and what was abstained

**Six months — ISSUED.** Share of the current top-50 court articles still in
the top-50 two mature quarters later: **0.672**, backtested on 5 rolling
folds. Wrong if it misses by more than 0.10.

**Twelve months — ISSUED AS A RANKED LIST.** Which articles outside the
current top-50 enter it. Base rate **0.045**, precision of a top-20 list
**0.31**, **lift 6.8889** over 5 folds. **RANK_ONLY, no probabilities** — the
forecasting programme already established that fold-to-fold precision varies
too widely for a calibrated number.

**Twenty-four months — ABSTAIN.** Ten mature quarters; a 24-month horizon is
eight of them, so a backtest would have at most two non-overlapping folds.
Temporal depth does not justify the horizon.

Both carry `REGIME_ASSUMPTION: no material detected regime break before target
maturity`, scored against the frozen regime detector — which cannot be used to
excuse a miss.

## 8. Which retrieval architecture survives law changing

Pseudo-future: build on history to a fold, score on the next mature quarter's
court citations, 5 folds.

| architecture | mean coverage | drift |
|---|--:|--:|
| **statute + doctrinal companions** | 0.953 | **-0.0108** |
| statute + current article ecology | 0.9417 | -0.0169 |
| time-aware recent window | 0.9287 | -0.0225 |
| statute only | 0.9273 | -0.0308 |
| speaker-aware hybrid | 0.9284 | -0.0343 |

Coverage alone rewards whichever index is biggest, so the column that matters
is **drift** — how much coverage an architecture loses as the law moves under
it. Adding the doctrinal companion layer loses the least; the speaker-aware
hybrid, which the earlier programme built, loses the most. Not a product
recommendation.

## 10. The live chain, and the surprise ledger

From this commit onward an event captured **prospectively** records milestones
as they occur — signal, first observable use, party use, court use, doctrinal
companion, operational core, retrieval impact — **stored, not sequenced**,
because the transition programme already showed an apparent ordering can be an
artefact of when the clock was started. Current prospective entries: **0**, on
purpose.

And the unknown-unknown watch: any recurring instrument, identity or formula
class outside the known families that crosses the repository's usual support
threshold opens a SURPRISE_LEDGER entry **first**, with the explanation
searched for afterwards. An entry with no explanation stays UNKNOWN.

## Can this repository answer the question today?

> What is happening in Saudi law now, what is beginning to change, what AI may
> change next, and what have we committed to expect before the answer arrives?

**Now:** fifty articles carry four fifths of visible commercial law-in-action;
the Commercial Courts Law and the Law of Evidence carry 0.7078 of all court
citations between them; two voices overlap on a quarter of their top fifty;
two judgments in five reach outside a statute and a third of what they reach
for is a named source.

**Beginning to change:** the Civil Transactions Law is newly visible, nine
codes and 76 articles are rising, 15 articles are newly visible, and five
doctrinal sources are gaining.

**What AI may change next:** unknown, and now framed so it can be answered.
The law of the observed forum already permits AI in its own procedures in four
explicit articles, no dispute in the corpus makes AI material, six competing
hypotheses are frozen with falsifiers, and every one of them waits on an
adoption event reaching this workflow.

**What we have committed to:** a six-month hold-rate forecast at 0.672, a
twelve-month ranked entry list at lift 6.8889, a 24-month abstention, seven
branches with falsifiers, seventeen AI issue families with watch conditions,
and a first-case capture schema armed against a base rate of zero.

The honest caveat on all of it: this is a nowcast of a record that lags real
time by about two years, and absence here is absence from published Ministry
of Justice commercial adjudication — never absence from Saudi law.

## Standing limitations

- **The observation lag.** Latest mature quarter 1446Q1, roughly 2024-07.
- **Publication timing is unresolved and now permanent.** No publication date
  per judgment; decision-to-publication lag cannot be separated from legal
  change. Recorded, not researched further.
- Momentum compares two adjacent four-quarter windows. It is not an
  extrapolation.
- The forum is commercial adjudication. The most active AI and data frontiers
  will not first appear here.
- Nothing in the anchor map is legal advice, and no outcome is predicted.
